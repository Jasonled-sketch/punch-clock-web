## 1. Steel in Taiwan: standard sizes, how it is sold, and 2025–2026 prices

### Takeaway
Mill/wholesale steel in Taiwan sat around NT$19.3–27/kg in Oct–Nov 2026 (hot-rolled flat ≈ NT$19.3–19.7k/ton traded; sections ≈ NT$25.5k/ton list) and was rising slightly; the only whole-sheet retail-type datapoints found imply about NT$35/kg for 4×8 black plate (undated) and NT$37–43/kg for 6 m square tube (2020 listings), so a 2026 small-buyer planning figure of roughly NT$35–45/kg is an estimate, not a quote. Steel is priced by weight (kg), sold as standard 4×8 尺 / 5×10 尺 sheets or 6 m lengths, with cut-to-size (零切) available and offcuts often charged to the buyer.

### Inferences
- **Sheet geometry [CALC]**: 4×8 尺 = 1220×2440 mm = 2.977 m²; 5×10 尺 = 1524×3048 mm = 4.645 m². Plate weight = 7.85 kg per mm per m² → 6 mm 47.1, 9 mm 70.7, 10 mm 78.5, 12 mm 94.2, 16 mm 125.6, 19 mm 149.2 kg/m².
- **Offcuts from one 4×8 sheet (1220×2440) [CALC]**:

  | Top size | Top area | Offcut pieces (one sensible cut plan) | Offcut area / share | Offcut weight at 10 mm |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 75×180 cm | 1.35 m² | 470×1800 + 1220×640 mm | 1.63 m² / 55% | 128 kg |
  | 90×180 cm | 1.62 m² | 320×1800 + 1220×640 mm | 1.36 m² / 46% | 107 kg |
  | 75×240 cm | 1.80 m² | 470×2440 strip | 1.15 m² / 39% | 90 kg |
  | 90×240 cm | 2.16 m² | 320×2440 strip | 0.78 m² / 26% | 61 kg |
  | 120×240 cm | 2.98 m² | none | 0 | 0 |

  A 90×240 top wastes least (26%) and its 320 mm strip is useful as a lower shelf, gussets, caster plates, or a tool tray; 75×180 throws away more than half the sheet unless the yard sells by cut weight. Because offcuts may be billed to the buyer (Mobile01), "90×240 or the full 120×240" is the economical choice when buying a whole sheet; 180 cm lengths only make sense where the yard sells 零切 by weight.
- **Planning price [EST]**: mill-level prices of NT$19.3–27/kg plus distributor margin, cutting and small-lot handling are consistent with the NT$35/kg estimate-table figure and the NT$37–43/kg tube listings. For Nov 2026 I would plan at **NT$35–45/kg for plate, tube, angle, channel and flat bar bought from a 鐵材行**, and NT$100+/kg for anything bought as small pre-cut pieces online. 2026 list prices rose roughly NT$1,800/ton in Q4, i.e. under NT$2/kg — small relative to the retail spread.
- The paving-plate figures imply only about NT$15–19/kg new and NT$8–12/kg used [CALC] if the 730 kg weight is right, which is below mill list price for new plate; a true 8 分 (25.4 mm) 5×16 尺 plate would weigh about 1,480 kg [CALC]. The source's weight or thickness convention is therefore doubtful — treat as unverified. Used paving/ship plate is nevertheless a plausible near-scrap-price source of thick plate, with the caveat that it is usually pitted and not flat.
- Where hobbyists buy [EST based on the listings above]: local 鐵材行 / 鋼鐵行 (whole sheets, 6 m sticks, shear/flame/plasma cut to size), laser shops (雷射切割代工) that will supply material, and auction listings on 露天/Yahoo for standard sticks; delivery of a whole sheet normally needs the yard's truck.

## 2. Weight calculations and handling feasibility

### Takeaway
A 10 mm top weighs about 106–170 kg and a 16 mm top about 170–270 kg for the candidate sizes, so nothing thicker than 6 mm in these sizes is a one- or two-person hand lift; Taiwan's labour rules classify manual carrying of 40 kg or more as heavy physical work.

### Cited Findings
- Taiwan's 重體力勞動作業勞工保護措施標準 lists "manual carrying or shouldering of objects weighing 40 kg or more" as heavy physical labour (and 20 kg or more for manual handling in thin-plate rolling work) — as quoted in [搬運作業人員健康體能保護手冊 (勞動部職安研究所版)](https://epc.stust.edu.tw/Sysid/epc/download/D實驗教學教材區/搬運作業人員健康體能保護手冊(勞動部職安研究所版).pdf) and [文化大學環安 PDF](https://esh.pccu.edu.tw/var/file/19/1019/img/625/337186510.pdf)
- Guidance that employers should assess manual-handling risk, provide mechanical aids, and use trained teams for team lifts (no kg figure given) — [香港勞工處 MHO guide](https://www.labour.gov.hk/tc/public/pdf/os/D/MHO-Employer.pdf)
- Supplier weights for comparison: 1219×2440 sheet at 10 mm = 235.59 kg, 12 mm = 282.71 kg — [金宏不銹鋼材](https://www.jinhong.com.tw/products_detail/28.htm)
- Square tube theoretical weights (50×50×3.0 = 4.44 kg/m; 75×75×3.0 = 6.79; 100×100×3.0 = 9.15; 100×100×4.0 = 12.0; 100×100×5.0 = 14.8) — [浚利鋼鐵](https://www.jiunli.com.tw/spec-pipe-ot-square.html)

### Inferences
- **Top weights [CALC], kg (area × thickness × 7.85)**:

  | Top | Area | 6 mm | 10 mm | 12 mm | 16 mm |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 75×180 cm | 1.35 m² | 64 | 106 | 127 | 170 |
  | 90×180 cm | 1.62 m² | 76 | 127 | 153 | 203 |
  | 75×240 cm | 1.80 m² | 85 | 141 | 170 | 226 |
  | 90×240 cm | 2.16 m² | 102 | 170 | 203 | 271 |
  | 120×240 cm (full 4×8) | 2.98 m² | 140 | 234 | 280 | 374 |
  | 152×305 cm (full 5×10) | 4.65 m² | 219 | 365 | 438 | 583 |

- **Square/rectangular tube kg/m [CALC], sharp-corner formula (A·B − (A−2t)(B−2t)) × 7.85; real tube with rounded corners is about 3–4% lighter** (the formula gives 4.43 for 50×50×3.0 against the supplier's 4.44, so it is a fair check):

  | Section | 2.3 mm | 3.2 mm | 4.5 mm |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 50×50 | 3.44 | 4.70 | 6.43 |
  | 75×75 | — | 7.21 | 9.96 |
  | 100×50 | — | 7.21 | 9.96 |
  | 100×100 | — | 9.73 | 13.49 |

  A 6 m stick therefore weighs about 28 kg (50×50×3.2), 43 kg (75×75×3.2 or 100×50×3.2), 58 kg (100×100×3.2), 60 kg (75×75×4.5), 81 kg (100×100×4.5).
- **Frame weight [CALC/EST]** for a 90×180×90 cm, 6-leg frame (6 legs ≈ 0.8 m; top ring 5.4 m + 2 cross-members 1.8 m; lower ring 5.4 m + cross-members 1.8 m ≈ 19–24 m of tube → 4 sticks of 6 m): 50×50×3.2 ≈ 90–113 kg; 75×75×3.2 ≈ 140–173 kg; 75×75×4.5 ≈ 190–240 kg. For a 240 cm table allow one more stick (5 sticks, about 26–30 m).
- **Complete table mass [EST]**: 90×180 with 10 mm top on 50×50×3.2 ≈ 230–250 kg; with 16 mm top on 75×75×4.5 ≈ 420–460 kg; 90×240 with 16 mm top ≈ 500–550 kg including hardware. Six casters rated 300–400 kg each or leveling casters rated ≥250 kg each are ample; the binding constraint is floor point load and rolling resistance, not caster rating.
- **Feasibility without a crane [EST]**: at the 40 kg-per-person heavy-labour threshold, a 6 mm 75×180 top (64 kg) is a two-person lift; a 10 mm top (106–170 kg) needs 3–5 people or, more sensibly, sliding/levering from the delivery truck onto the frame with pipe rollers, an engine hoist, chain block, or the yard's truck-mounted crane (吊卡). 12–16 mm tops (127–270 kg) should be treated as machine-handled: have the yard deliver by crane truck and set the plate directly on the finished frame, or build the frame upside-down on the plate and flip with a hoist. Splitting the top into two plates (e.g. two 90×120 pieces: 85 kg each at 10 mm) halves the lift but adds a seam.

### Gaps
- No Taiwanese source gives a numeric team-lift limit for steel plate; the 40 kg figure is a legal classification threshold, not a safe-lift limit.
- No source found for the cost of crane-truck (吊卡車) delivery of a single sheet in Taiwan.
- JIS/CNS table weights for 3.2 mm and 4.5 mm wall tube were not retrieved (supplier table snippets only showed 2.0/3.0/4.0/5.0 mm).

## 4. Laser-cut custom fixture tops and alternatives

### Takeaway

- General top-thickness guidance: 5–6 mm for light work and tacking, 9–12 mm for precise work or hammering (Japanese DIY guide); 3/8" (10 mm) all-round, 1/4" light, 1/2" heavy; 5/8" (16 mm) holes are the usual jig-table standard — [trunktrunk.click](https://trunktrunk.click/yousetsudaiwojiukurikatakanzengaido.html), [wigglesworth.com](https://wigglesworth.com/feeds/blog/welding-table-beginners)

### Inferences
- **Laser cost model for the hole grid [CALC/EST]** at NT$100/m (10 mm): each Ø16 hole = 50.3 mm of cut.
  - 90×180 cm, 50 mm pitch: about 17×35 = 595 holes → 29.9 m + 5.4 m perimeter ≈ 35 m → **≈ NT$3,500 cut-path charge + 595 pierces**.
  - 90×180 cm, 100 mm pitch: about 9×18 = 162 holes → 8.1 m + 5.4 m ≈ 13.5 m → **≈ NT$1,350 + 162 pierces**.
  - 90×240 cm, 50 mm pitch: about 17×47 = 799 holes → 40.2 m + 6.6 m ≈ 47 m → **≈ NT$4,700 + 799 pierces**.
  - If a pierce in 10–12 mm plate costs NT$5–20 [EST, unsourced], pierces add about NT$800–3,200 (100 mm pitch) or NT$3,000–16,000 (50 mm pitch). A sensible planning range for laser work on a 10–12 mm top is therefore **NT$2,500–5,000 at 100 mm pitch and NT$7,000–20,000 at 50 mm pitch**, excluding material — to be replaced by a real quote. 12–16 mm plate will be priced higher per metre ("厚板另議").
  - A 100 mm pitch cuts the hole count by roughly 73% and is the obvious economy choice; D16 clamps work on either pitch.
- Laser-cut holes in 10–16 mm plate carry taper and heat-affected hardened edges; hobby builds commonly accept the fit or ream afterwards [EST — general fabrication knowledge, not sourced here]. Local heat input from hundreds of holes can also bow a plate, which is why thin laser-cut tops are ribbed underneath (as the 4.75 mm commercial example shows).
- Buying a mag drill (≈NT$27,500) for a one-off table costs more than outsourcing the laser work; it only pays if the tool is wanted anyway. Drilling 160–600 holes of Ø16 in 10–16 mm plate by mag drill is also many hours of work [EST].
- A slatted top (flat bar 100×12 or 100×16 with gaps for clamps) avoids laser cost entirely and can be bought at section-steel prices (flat bar ex-mill NT$24.5–25/kg, see section 1), but individual bars must be shimmed flat.
