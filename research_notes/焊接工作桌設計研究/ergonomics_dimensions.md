# Welding table (焊接工作桌) ergonomics and dimensions for a 173 cm user in Taiwan

> Method note for the report writer: page fetching was blocked in this session (ccohs.gc.ca and tractorbynet.com did not resolve; they are outside the session's allowed network domains). Every finding below therefore comes from web-search result summaries and snippets, not from reading the full pages. Quotes are as the search tool returned them. ## 1. What working height suits a 173 cm person for standing MIG/CO2 welding?

### Takeaway
Ergonomics sources put standing work between waist and elbow height, with light work 5–10 cm below the elbow; welders' forum rules and commercial tables cluster at 86–97 cm (34–38 in), and the one poster of the user's height (5'8") uses 89 cm (35 in). For a 173 cm user with an estimated elbow height of about 111 cm barefoot, a table top of roughly 88–90 cm with levelling feet is the best-supported fixed height.

### Cited Findings

Ergonomics guidance
- CCOHS welding guidance: "Position the welding item as flat as possible, on a horizontal surface, between waist and elbow height." For seated work: "Work with material slightly below elbow level when working in a sitting position." — [CCOHS Welding - Ergonomics](https://ccohs.gc.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/welding/ergonomics.html)
- CCOHS standing-work heights relative to elbow height: precision work (writing, electronic assembly) about 5 cm above elbow height with elbow support; light work (assembly line, mechanical jobs) about 5–10 cm below elbow height; heavy work demanding downward force 20–40 cm below elbow height. — [CCOHS Working in a Standing Position](https://ccohs.gc.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/standing/standing_basic.html)
- A retailer guide restates the same bands in inches: about 2 in above the elbow for precision, 2–4 in below for light work, 8–16 in below for heavy work; it also cites OSHA as suggesting 2–4 in above elbow height for close visual work (attribution uncertain, secondary source). — [Zoro work bench height guide](https://www.zoro.com/resourcehub/work-bench-height-guide/)
- OSHA's prefabrication welding page recommends height-adjustable tables and jigs that hold the piece in a position that lets employees keep neutral postures. — [OSHA eTool: Prefabrication, Welding](https://www.osha.gov/etools/electrical-contractors/prefabrication/welding)
- UK HSE: for most standing tasks the preferred working height is below elbow height; only delicate, manipulative tasks need to be above it (attribution uncertain: the search summary named HSE without linking the exact page; this is the closest HSE page returned). — [HSE Reducing awkward postures](https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/uld/art/posture.htm)

Anthropometry (Taiwan)
- Lin, Wang & Wang, Applied Ergonomics 35 (2004) 173–178: Taiwanese adult male stature 1699 mm; elbow height 1088 mm for males and 1007 mm for females. The search summary labels the 1088 mm figure "elbow height (straight arm)" and could not confirm the flexed-arm value the paper also defines. The copy found is on Scribd, not the journal page. — [Scribd copy of Lin, Wang & Wang 2004](https://www.scribd.com/document/233806745/Sd-Article)
- A 2021 study (Chen et al., IJERPH) gives newer Taiwanese adult anthropometric reference data from a large sample, but the snippets did not include elbow height. — [PMC8306797](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8306797)

### Inferences
- Elbow height for this user: scaling the Taiwanese male ratio (1088 / 1699 = 0.640) to 173 cm gives about 111 cm barefoot. Work shoes add roughly 2–3 cm (my assumption, not sourced), so about 113–114 cm shod. The user should measure their own elbow height in work shoes; it takes one minute and removes this uncertainty.
- Converting the rules to numbers for a 111 cm barefoot elbow:

| Rule | Offset from elbow | Height for this user (barefoot) |
|---|---|---|
| CCOHS precision work | +5 cm | 116 cm |
| CCOHS light work | −5 to −10 cm | 101–106 cm |
| WeldingWeb standing workstation | −15 to −25 cm | 86–96 cm (88–98 cm in shoes) |
| CCOHS heavy work | −20 to −40 cm | 71–91 cm |

- The CCOHS numbers are heights of the hands and the work, not of the table top. The weld sits above the table by the height of the part plus any fixture. That is why welders' table heights (86–97 cm) sit 10–20 cm below the CCOHS light-work band, and the two sets of numbers agree once the part height is subtracted.
- Scaling the forum data points by stature (table height / body height = 0.51 to 0.57 across the five posters) gives 89–99 cm for 173 cm. The single same-height data point is 89 cm.
- Suggested number: a table top at 88–90 cm, with levelling feet that give a few cm of adjustment. This suits flat frame work in 25–50 mm square tube, where the weld is only 3–10 cm above the top. It is a compromise for upright assemblies such as display stands and tripod legs, whose joints can sit 20–40 cm above the top and would ergonomically want a lower surface.
- Seated welding: CCOHS wants the work slightly below seated elbow level. The low end of adjustable commercial tables (71–76 cm) matches that use; an 88–90 cm table needs a tall stool instead.

### Gaps
- I could not read the CCOHS, OSHA or HSE pages directly, so the wording above is as quoted by the search tool. The HSE attribution in particular needs checking.
- No 勞動部職安署 / 勞安所 anthropometric table for standing elbow height was found. The only Taiwanese elbow figure is the 2004 paper via a Scribd copy, with the arm posture of the measurement unclear.
- A secondary summary attributed absolute heights to NIOSH (precision work 37–43 in from the floor), but I could not identify the page or the light and heavy bands, so it is not used.
- "Belt-buckle height", "wrist height" and "knuckle height" rules were searched for but did not surface in the snippets; only elbow, forearm-parallel and belly-button rules did.
- No Taiwanese or Chinese-language source giving a recommended welding table height was found. Chinese 3D-table vendors list height as "customized".
- Japanese search returned no ergonomic rule for 溶接台 height, only one product spec.

## 2. How does table height interact with fixtures and a chop saw, and is adjustable height worth it?

### Takeaway
Builders treat the chop saw as a reason to lower a shelf, not the table: the saw goes on a drop-down or slide-in shelf set so its bed is flush with the table top, which then acts as the stock support. OSHA and most adjustable commercial tables favour adjustable height, and forum posters recommend adjustable legs for fine-tuning.

### Cited Findings
- Flush-mount method: "Measure from the cutting surface down to top of table for your offset, then put a shelf at that distance down for your saw to set in." — [Pirate4x4: Flush Mount Chop Saw](https://www.pirate4x4.com/threads/flush-mount-chop-saw.922565/) (attribution uncertain between this and the Garage Journal thread below)
- Builders fabricate a platform attached to the saw that folds up under the welding table and, in use, is level with the table top: "This allows the table to be used as support for cutting long stock." — [Garage Journal: welding table with chop saw mounted flush](https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/help-locating-a-post-or-picture-of-a-welding-table-has-a-chop-saw-mounted-flush-in-it.313364/) (attribution uncertain)
- Pivoting mount: one builder's saw was flush with the table in use and pivoted down out of the way when a pin was pulled. Another mounted the saw on steel tube that slides into receivers at the table ends, keeping the whole top free. — [Garage Journal: Starting a chop saw table](https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/starting-a-chop-saw-table.84630/) (attribution uncertain)
- Receiver-tube shelf: "Instead of the swinging shelf, use receiver tubing with lock nuts and slide the shelf in. Position the receivers so saw table is height of welding table so welding table will support material. The reciver tubes can also be used for table slide in extensions." — [Green Tractor Talk: Welding table ideas?](https://www.greentractortalk.com/threads/welding-table-ideas.195672/) (attribution uncertain)
- Trade-offs of a folding saw stand: it would cost "18+" of table top" (46 cm) when the saw folds down, and "you will still need a tripod or other support on the other end" of long stock. — [WeldingWeb: Welding table with folding saw stand](https://www.weldingweb.com/threads/welding-table-with-folding-saw-stand.707719/)
- Leave the bottom open under the chop saw so sparks and debris fall out. — [Pirate4x4: Flush Mount Chop Saw](https://www.pirate4x4.com/threads/flush-mount-chop-saw.922565/) (attribution uncertain)
- OSHA recommends height-adjustable tables and jigs for welding prefabrication work. — [OSHA eTool: Prefabrication, Welding](https://www.osha.gov/etools/electrical-contractors/prefabrication/welding)
- Commercial tables commonly ship with an adjustment range rather than one height: Siegmund 71–102 cm or 76–107 cm, BuildPro 75–95 cm, Flextur 89–104 cm. — [Siegmund System 16](https://siegmund.com/en-us/Imperial-Series/Products/Welding-Tables-System-16,6555.php); [Zoro BuildPro listing](https://www.zoro.com/i/G107200133); [Zoro Flextur listing](https://zoro.com/flextur-welding-table-48-d-35-h-48-w-metal-78909515/i/G007630597)
- Siegmund sells a System 16 mobile lifting weld table, 47x31 in (about 120 x 80 cm). — [Trick-Tools Siegmund mobile lifting table](https://www.trick-tools.com/2-169910-Siegmund-System-16-Mobile-Lifting-Weld-Table-47x31-inch-15246)
- A Taiwanese DIY builder put adjustable feet under the legs because the floor is uneven ("腳架下面做調整腳的設計，以免地面不平"). — [小間製作所 Fineworks.tw: 焊接工作桌製作](http://www.fineworks.tw/modules/xforum/viewtopic.php?post_id=498)

### Inferences
- A chop saw sitting on the table top raises the cut by the saw's bed height and takes up top area. The documented fix is a shelf below the top at exactly the saw's bed height, so the saw does not change the choice of table height.
- If the user plans to use fixture blocks, clamps or a raised sub-plate regularly, that argues for the lower end of the height range (about 86–88 cm). Flat work directly on the top argues for the upper end (about 90 cm).
- For a budget build, threaded levelling feet or telescoping legs with pin holes give most of the benefit of adjustability. A lifting mechanism is hard to justify for a table whose top alone weighs over 100 kg (see section 5).

### Gaps
- I found no sourced figure for the bed height of a typical 355 mm (14 in) abrasive chop saw or cold saw; the user should measure their own saw.
- No source quantified typical fixture stack height on a 16 mm system table.
- No hobby-level discussion of the cost or mechanism for adjustable height (scissor lift, telescoping legs) was captured.

## 3. Width: 75 cm vs 90 cm (or 100, 120 cm)

### Takeaway
Forum opinion favours about 90 cm (36 in, "3 ft") as the home-shop width: one builder planning 30 in (76 cm) was advised to go to at least 36 in, and 3 ft is described as enough at home because wider tops are hard to reach across and walk around. A minority regrets 3 ft as slightly narrow and would choose 4 ft (122 cm).

### Cited Findings
- A builder planning a 30 in (76 cm) deep top was told: "If you haven't already purchased the plate I'd be inclined to go at least 36" wide and perhaps shorter if you're concerned about space." — [TractorByNet: Building a Shop / Weld Table](https://www.tractorbynet.com/forums/goto/post?id=5056407)
- Hobart forum (2004): "a top 4 ft wide is certainly plenty, at home unless you have a specific reason 3 ft is enough and the reason being when its too big it makes it too hard to reach across and walk around." — [Hobart Weld Talk: Opinion on Welding Table Concept](https://weldtalk.hobartwelders.com/forum/weld-talk-topic-archive/welding-projects/8946-opinion-on-welding-table-concept)
- A Garage Journal user with a 3 ft top: "3 ft is a bit narrow, if I built a new top its the number I would pick" (the number being 4 ft, per the search summary). — [Garage Journal: Maximum overhang for welding table](https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/maximum-overhang-for-welding-table.430883/)
- Other owners: a 3x5 ft (91 x 152 cm) table "was sufficient most of the time"; a 3x4 ft (91 x 122 cm) owner said "I've never wanted for more, and I've used the inner limit a lot." — [TractorByNet: Welding Table](https://www.tractorbynet.com/forums/threads/welding-table.204056/post-2348015) (attribution uncertain)
- A general sizing guide says most single-user weld tables are 30–36 in wide (76–91 cm) and 48–72 in long (122–183 cm), smaller for tight floor space or small TIG work and longer for frame straightening. Weakly sourced. — [Coohom DIY weld table guide](https://www.coohom.com/article/diy-weld-table-your-essential-guide-to-building-a-sturdy-work-surface)
- A trade-school guide: 2–3 ft in each direction for smaller workpieces; 4 ft or more in length and width "to work on big hunks of metal". — [Tulsa Welding School: How to Choose a Welding Table](https://www.tws.edu/blog/welding/how-to-choose-a-welding-table/)
- A blog calls about 3 ft wide enough for comfortable working with materials in arm's reach. Low-authority source. — [Meaningful Spaces](https://www.meaningfulspaces.com/what-is-the-best-height-for-a-welding-table/)
- Commercial metric widths seen: Siegmund System 16 tables at 1000x500, 1200x1200, 2000x1000 and 2000x1200 mm; BuildPro at 47.2 in (1200 mm) deep; Chinese 3D tables at 2000x1000, 1000x1500 and 2000x1500 mm. — [Weld-Ready Siegmund 2000x1200](https://weld-ready.com/products/siegmund-welding-table-2000x1200-system-16); [Zoro BuildPro listing](https://www.zoro.com/i/G107200133); [Lemar D28 2000x1000 listing](https://lemarwelding.en.made-in-china.com/product/KJPpMhVOLyck/China-High-Precision-D28-2000-1000-200mm-Modular-Welding-Tables.html)
- A Taiwanese DIY fixture-table build used a 1000 x 700 mm top. — [小間製作所 Fineworks.tw: 焊接工作桌製作](http://www.fineworks.tw/modules/xforum/viewtopic.php?post_id=498)
- A Taiwanese DIY workbench/welding table video lists a 2400 x 1200 x 5 mm steel sheet as the top material. — [YouTube 週末黑手黨 WEEK3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TOsiuYMeY1U)

### Inferences
- Between the user's two options, the evidence leans to 90 cm. It is the most commonly endorsed home-shop width, and nobody in the captured threads recommended going down to 75 cm; 76 cm appears only as the low end of a range or as a plan someone was advised to widen.
- 75 cm is defensible if the table stands against a wall and is worked from one side only, since the whole depth must then be reached from the front. With access from both sides, 90 cm means reaching only 45 cm to the centre.
- For the user's stated work (display stands, tube frames), a 90 cm width lets a frame up to roughly 80 cm wide lie fully supported with clamps on both long edges, which a 75 cm top cannot do.
- Widths of 100–120 cm match commercial fixture tables but are sized for walk-around production use; the "hard to reach across" complaint applies to them in a home shop.

### Gaps
- No source directly compared 750 mm with 900 mm, and no ergonomics source for maximum comfortable forward reach was captured, so the reach argument rests on forum opinion.
- Nothing was found on passing the table through doors or on Taiwanese door widths. The user should measure the doorway and lift access if the table must ever move; a removable top solves this regardless of width.
- No Mobile01 or PTT discussion of welding table width was found.

## 4. Length: 180 cm vs 240 cm, and how to support long stock

### Takeaway
No source argues that a hobby table must be 240 cm; guides put most single-user tables at 122–183 cm long, and one poster warns that an 8 ft (244 cm) table is not rigid in the middle without stiffening. The documented way to handle long pieces is a shorter table plus receiver-tube slide-in extensions, pull-out wings, or a separate stand at the far end.

### Cited Findings
- Most single-user weld tables are 48–72 in long (122–183 cm), longer for frame straightening. Weakly sourced. — [Coohom DIY weld table guide](https://www.coohom.com/article/diy-weld-table-your-essential-guide-to-building-a-sturdy-work-surface)
- Rigidity warning: "at 8' length that table will not be rigid if working in the middle." Another builder added angle stiffeners under the top every 16 in (41 cm) and said that held up to heavy hammering. — [TractorByNet: Receivers on welding tables](https://www.tractorbynet.com/forums/threads/receivers-on-welding-tables.391964/page-2) (attribution uncertain between this thread and "Building a Shop / Weld Table")
- One builder justified a large table because "I build a lot of livestock panels, gates and other long things." — [TractorByNet: Building a Shop / Weld Table](https://www.tractorbynet.com/forums/goto/post?id=5112990) (attribution uncertain)
- Receiver tubes are welded into table frames so a vise or bender can be attached and swapped without disturbing a project laid out on the table. — [TractorByNet: Receivers on welding tables](https://www.tractorbynet.com/forums/threads/receivers-on-welding-tables.391964/post-5009601)
- Receiver details: the typical 2 in receiver "is pretty sloppy"; one builder removed the play with plug welds on the stinger ground to a tight fit; standard receiver stock is 2.530 in OD with 0.250 in wall (64.3 mm OD, 6.35 mm wall), which accepts 2 in (50.8 mm) heavy-wall tube; a pin near the outer end acts as a stop. — [TractorByNet: Receivers on welding tables](https://www.tractorbynet.com/forums/threads/receivers-on-welding-tables.391964/page-2)
- "The reciver tubes can also be used for table slide in extensions." — [Green Tractor Talk: Welding table ideas?](https://www.greentractortalk.com/threads/welding-table-ideas.195672/) (attribution uncertain)
- A homemade table has the saw on the lower shelf with "left and right pull out extension wings to support long pieces of stock when needed." — [HomemadeTools.net: The Ultimate Welding Table](https://www.homemadetools.net/forum/ultimate-welding-table-74310)
- Even with the saw at table height, "you will still need a tripod or other support on the other end." — [WeldingWeb: Welding table with folding saw stand](https://www.weldingweb.com/threads/welding-table-with-folding-saw-stand.707719/)
- Commercial lengths: Siegmund System 16 up to 2000 mm and 118 in (3000 mm); BuildPro up to 94.5 in (2400 mm); Chinese 3D tables at 2000, 3000 and 4000 mm. — [Trick-Tools Siegmund 118x59 in](https://www.trick-tools.com/4-160050.X7.2-Siegmund-System-16-Welding-Table-118x59-inch-13230); [Zoro BuildPro 94.5 in listing](https://zoro.com/buildpro-welding-table-472-d-433-h-945-l-t28-2412xq-b1/i/G107200096); [Made-in-China 2000x4000 listing](https://m.made-in-china.com/amp/product/2000-4000mm-16mm-System-Fixturing-Welding-Table-with-Legs-2022182442.html)

### Inferences
- A 180 cm table with slide-out extensions is the better-supported choice for this user. The current work is small, the rigidity warning applies to 240 cm, and several independent builders use slide-in tubes or pull-out wings for the occasional long piece.
- A practical extension design from the sources: two square tubes run lengthwise under the top as receivers, with smaller telescoping tubes that pull out of each end and carry a cross-bar set flush with the table top. Pulling each end out 60–100 cm supports 3–4 m stock on a 180 cm table. The forum caution about slop means the fit should be shimmed or tightened with a clamping bolt.
- Local steel service centres commonly stock 4x8 ft plate (1219 x 2438 mm), as the Taiwanese build's 2400 x 1200 sheet suggests. Cutting a 180 cm top from such a sheet leaves an offcut of about 64 cm that can become an extension wing or a second small table. A 240 cm top uses the full sheet length.
- If the table is 240 cm, the frame needs intermediate legs or cross-members; the sourced figure is stiffeners about every 40 cm under the top.

### Gaps
- No documented commercial or DIY design with dimensions for slide-out extension rails on a welding table was captured, only descriptions.
- Nothing was found on modular two-table setups (two 120 cm tables bolted in line), though Siegmund-style tables are designed to be joined.
- No source compared 180 cm with 240 cm directly.
- Roller stands were not discussed in the captured results beyond the generic "tripod or other support".

## 5. Table top: thickness, solid vs slatted vs fixture-hole, overhang, flatness

### Takeaway
For light fabrication, forum consensus is 3/8 in (about 10 mm) as the cost/weight/flatness balance and 1/2 in (about 12 mm) if the table will be hammered on or drilled as a fixture top; commercial 16 mm-hole tables use 12–14 mm plate on a 50 mm grid. Overhang recommendations run from 2.5–4 cm (minimum for clamping) to 7.5–10 cm (matching clamp throat depth).

### Cited Findings

Thickness
- AWS forum: for a fabrication table, 3/8 in to 1/2 in (9.5–12.7 mm) avoids heat distortion. — [AWS Forum](https://app.aws.org/forum/topic_show.pl?pid=26159)
- "if I were to build a welding table I would like to go 1/2" thick but with money the way it is it will be 3/8's." Another poster hammers on a 3/8 in top "with a 3# sledge all the time without any problems." — [TractorByNet: Welding table top?](https://tractorbynet.com/forums/threads/welding-table-top.347786/)
- "1/2" minimum for the top is a safe bet"; others in the same threads use 1/4 in (6.4 mm) plate for "all kinds of welding" and 1/8 in (3.2 mm) tread plate that "was never perfectly flat but it's flat enough for me." — [TractorByNet: welding table top what thickness needed](https://www.tractorbynet.com/forums/threads/welding-table-top-what-thickness-needed.271636)
- A table maker's FAQ compares 1/4, 3/8 and 1/2 in tops; the search summary's reading is that 3/8 in is more likely to be flat enough for most users than thinner plate. — [Texas Metal Works FAQ](https://texasmetalworks.com/faq/what-table-thickness-is-best-1-4-3-8-or-1-2-inch)
- Price point from a forum thread (US, undated): USD 225 for 3/8 in versus USD 295 for 1/2 in in a 4x8 ft sheet. — [TractorByNet: Welding table top?](https://tractorbynet.com/forums/threads/welding-table-top.347786/)
- Welding tables generally use thick steel tops of 10–50 mm on a heavy frame. — [Wikipedia: Welding table](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Welding_table)
- Taiwanese DIY examples: 10 mm laser-cut plate with 16 mm holes at 50 mm pitch on a 1000 x 700 mm top; and a 5 mm sheet on a 50x50x3 mm square-tube frame for a combined workbench/welding table. — [小間製作所 Fineworks.tw](http://www.fineworks.tw/modules/xforum/viewtopic.php?post_id=498); [YouTube 週末黑手黨 WEEK3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TOsiuYMeY1U)

Fixture-hole systems

Solid vs slatted vs holes
- Slotted tops let you clamp workpieces directly to the surface anywhere; solid tops make it hard to hold parts flat against the surface. — [Texas Metal Works: Are slotted tops better than solid tops?](https://texasmetalworks.com/faq/are-slotted-tops-better-than-solid-tops)
- Slats can be swapped for versions with different hole patterns, but changing them means re-levelling the table. — [Garage Journal: Welding fixture table types to fabricate](https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/welding-fixture-table-types-to-fabricate.543903/post-11081387)
- A drilled 1/2 in plate developed a crown of nearly 1/8 in (3 mm) after drilling, per one builder; 1/2 in is called a practical minimum for a fixture top. — [Fireball Tool forum: Avoid these mistakes when you build a fixture welding table](https://fireball.discourse.group/t/avoid-these-mistakes-when-you-build-a-fixture-welding-table/2902) (attribution uncertain)
- Flatness of the top is described as critical to stop workpieces distorting during welding. — [Coohom DIY welding tables](https://www.coohom.com/article/diy-welding-tables-build-your-custom-workspace-today) (attribution uncertain, weak source)

Overhang
- On a 30 x 56 in table with a 1/4 in stainless top: "1" overhang is plenty for 1/4" plate and it is just enough for clamping." Another suggestion: 1.5 in (3.8 cm) on the front and both sides. — [Canadian Hobby Metalworkers: Welding tabletop overhang](https://canadianhobbymetalworkers.com/threads/welding-tabletop-overhang.12166)
- A 3 x 4 ft, 1/4 in top has "about a 4" overhang which is about as much reach as most of my clamps have." Another builder planned 3 in (7.6 cm) on all four sides. — [Garage Journal: Maximum overhang for welding table](https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/maximum-overhang-for-welding-table.430883/)
- Alternative to overhang: build the frame at full table size and raise the top off the frame on pedestals, giving clamp access everywhere except at the pedestals. Size the gap by taking "the largest clamp you plan on using" and measuring the height needed between top and frame at the deepest throat you want. — [Garage Journal: Weld/fab table top stand off](https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/weld-fab-table-top-stand-off.465190/)
- Stability caution: with a traditional 2–3 in overhang, "my frame would be narrower than its height." — [Garage Journal: Weld/fab table top stand off](https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/weld-fab-table-top-stand-off.465190/)

### Inferences
- Thickness for this user: 10 mm solid plate is the best-supported budget choice for square-tube work, and 12 mm if the top will be drilled or laser-cut with 16 mm holes, since commercial 16 mm systems use 12–14 mm plate and a drilled 12.7 mm plate was reported to crown. 6 mm works for welding but the sources tie thinner plate to poorer flatness. 16 mm and thicker match the 28 mm systems and are beyond what light fabrication needs.
- Top weights, computed from steel at 7850 kg/m3 (standard density, my calculation):

| Top size | 6 mm | 10 mm | 12 mm | 16 mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 75 x 180 cm | 64 kg | 106 kg | 127 kg | 170 kg |
| 90 x 180 cm | 76 kg | 127 kg | 153 kg | 203 kg |
| 75 x 240 cm | 85 kg | 141 kg | 170 kg | 226 kg |
| 90 x 240 cm | 102 kg | 170 kg | 203 kg | 271 kg |

- If the user wants fixture holes, the metric 16 mm hole on a 50 mm grid is the right system for Taiwan: it matches Siegmund System 16, the Chinese D16 tables and the one Taiwanese DIY build found, so low-cost D16 clamps and pins will fit. A full grid on 90 x 180 cm is about 630 holes, which makes laser cutting by the steel supplier (as the Taiwanese builder did) the realistic route. Holes over only part of the top is a cheaper middle path.
- Overhang: 5 cm all round is a reasonable middle of the sourced 2.5–10 cm range and suits common F-clamps and locking C-clamps; 7.5–10 cm on the front edge if deep-throat clamps are used. On a 75 cm wide top a large overhang narrows the leg frame noticeably, which is a further point in favour of 90 cm.
- Slatted tops give the best clamp access for the least money, but the user's small parts (stand feet, short tube pieces) would fall through or sit unevenly across gaps; a solid or holed plate suits small-part work better.
